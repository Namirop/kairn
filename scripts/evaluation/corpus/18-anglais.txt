Survivorship bias is the error of concentrating on the entities that made it past a selection process while overlooking those that did not. The classic case is Abraham Wald's work on returning bombers during the Second World War: the armour should go where the returning planes were not hit, because the planes hit in those places never came back at all. The absence of data is itself the data. The same reasoning explains why lists of habits shared by successful founders are close to worthless — nobody surveys the founders who followed identical habits and failed.
