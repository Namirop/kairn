In Spanish, ser and estar both translate as "to be" but they are not
used in the same cases. Ser is for what is permanent or defining:
identity, origin, profession, stable characteristics. Soy francés, es
médico, la mesa es de madera. Estar is for what is temporary or
situational: the state of the moment, position, the result of a
change. Estoy cansado, está en casa, la sopa está fría. The same
sentence changes meaning with the verb: "es aburrido" means he is
boring, "está aburrido" means he is bored.
